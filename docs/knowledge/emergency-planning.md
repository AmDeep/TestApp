# Emergency preparedness and household continuity

A household emergency plan identifies local hazards, alerts, evacuation routes, meeting points, contacts, medication and essential supplies, pet needs and communication alternatives. Keep printed information available if devices or power fail. Follow local emergency-management instructions during active incidents. Do not delay evacuation to collect property or complete an app workflow.

## Practical workflow
1. Define the decision or problem in plain language. Record the room, asset, date, symptoms or observations, and what outcome matters.
2. Separate verified facts from assumptions. Record measurement units, source, age and confidence. Mark unknown values as unknown instead of filling gaps.
3. Identify immediate safety concerns first. Stop work and contact emergency services or a qualified professional when there is imminent danger, suspected gas, fire, electrical shock, structural instability or hazardous exposure.
4. Compare low-disruption options before irreversible work. Consider cost, maintenance, accessibility, permissions, waste, energy, durability and effects on other building systems.
5. Confirm local requirements and manufacturer instructions. Jurisdiction, climate, property type and product model can change the correct answer.
6. Record the chosen action, responsible person, budget range, approvals, evidence and review date. Revisit the result after the work or seasonal change.
